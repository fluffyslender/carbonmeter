## 1. Executive Summary & Value Proposition

### 1.1 The wedge, stated precisely

A remote-first company has no offices, no fleet, and no industrial process. Its Scope 1 is frequently a literal zero and its Scope 2 is a rounding error. This does not make it low-carbon — it makes it **structurally concentrated**. The entire footprint collapses into Scope 3, and inside Scope 3 it collapses into **Category 6, Business Travel**, because the company has replaced the office with the aeroplane.

Worked reference case — 120 FTE, distributed, one global all-hands plus two regional offsites per year:

| Source | tCO₂e/yr | Share |
|---|---|---|
| **Cat 6 — global all-hands** (air 179, hotel 10, ground 3) | **192** | 35% |
| **Cat 6 — two regional offsites** | **66** | 12% |
| **Cat 6 — client/conference/sales travel** | **55** | 10% |
| **Cat 6 — recruiting travel** | **8** | 1% |
| Cat 1 — cloud, SaaS, professional services, marketing | 140 | 25% |
| Cat 7 — home-working energy, residual commuting | 55 | 10% |
| Cat 2 — laptops & capital goods | 25 | 5% |
| Cat 4/5 — upstream logistics, waste | 6 | 1% |
| Scope 2 — hub/coworking allocation | 4 | <1% |
| Scope 1 | 0 | 0% |
| **Total** | **551** | |
| **Category 6 total** | **321** | **58%** |

*Indicative planning factors. Air computed at ≈0.16 kgCO₂e/pkm short-haul economy and ≈0.12 long-haul economy including radiative forcing, +9% great-circle uplift for indirect routing, hotel at ≈20 kgCO₂e/room-night (Iberia). Replace with the exact DESNZ 2026 workbook values before any client-facing use.*

**The headline that sells the company: 58% of this company's climate footprint is created by approximately four calendar decisions per year.** Where the all-hands happens. How often it happens. How long people stay. Which cabin they fly in. Nobody in the business owns those four decisions as carbon decisions — they sit with a People or Ops lead optimising for cost, culture and venue availability, on a six-to-ten week planning horizon, with no carbon input whatsoever until after the invoices land.

Three compounding failures follow:

1. **The decision is made before the data exists.** Every measurement product on the market tells them what an offsite cost them in March, after they booked it in January. Retrospective accounting cannot change an itinerary. The lever is a *forecast at venue-shortlist stage*, and effectively nobody sells one.
2. **The data arrives in the worst possible shape.** There is usually no Travel Management Company. Travel lands as 200 reimbursement rows in Pleo, Payhawk or Expensify — merchant name, amount, date — from which no distance-based calculation is possible without reconstruction. So it gets spend-averaged, which produces a number that is both wrong and unauditable, and which *rewards buying cheap flights* rather than flying less.
3. **Under SBTi rules this category alone nearly decides their target.** Where Scope 3 exceeds 40% of total emissions, near-term targets must cover ≥67% of Scope 3. At 58% of total, Category 6 is ~64% of this company's Scope 3 — so Category 6 is not one category among fifteen. It is very nearly the whole obligation, and it is the one they measure worst.

### 1.2 Why they buy — commercial pressure, not legal fear

Post-Omnibus, this client has **no direct legal disclosure duty and never will**. Say so openly; it is a trust-builder and every competitor still pitching CSRD panic to 120-person companies will be caught out. Four real forcing functions remain, all commercial:

- **Enterprise procurement.** Their large customers *are* in scope and must report Category 1 purchased goods and services. Supplier data requests are now a standing feature of enterprise deals. A supplier who answers in four days with a methodologically sound figure closes faster than one who stalls for six weeks.
- **Investors.** Article 8/9 SFDR funds and most climate-conscious VCs impose portfolio reporting covenants that are contractual, not statutory, and therefore untouched by Omnibus.
- **Certifications and commitments already signed.** B Corp recertification, SME Climate Hub / Race to Zero pledges, EcoVadis and CDP Supply Chain scores. Many signed a halve-by-2030 commitment and never built a baseline.
- **Talent.** For a remote-first employer, the offsite *is* the employer brand. "We fly 120 people to Lisbon and call it carbon neutral" ages very badly with the engineers they are trying to hire — and as of two days ago, saying it is unlawful in consumer-facing contexts.

### 1.3 The offering

**Done-for-you Category 6 measurement and destination impact design, delivered by humans, sold with no software licence and no commission from anyone.**

Four things define it against everything adjacent:

1. **VSME-complete, not CSRD-cosplay.** Every deliverable maps to the VSME module set — the legal ceiling of what any customer may demand of them — and secondarily to ESRS E1-6 for the enterprise customers who will ask in ESRS language anyway. One document answers every questionnaire, and gives them standing to **decline** anything beyond it. "We will make you the easiest supplier in your customer's Scope 3, and give you the legal basis to refuse everything else" is the strongest sentence available in this market.
2. **Gross emissions and local contribution are structurally severed.** Not a disclaimer — an architecture. Different sections, different units, different invoices, different counterparties, and money for local projects never passes through the LDA. Section 2 explains why this is simultaneously the anti-greenwashing position, the licensing-avoidance position and the tax position.
3. **Forecast before booking, not audit after invoicing.** The offsite pre-mortem — modelled emissions for three candidate destinations before the venue deposit is paid — is the only part of the product that changes the outcome rather than describing it. It is also what converts a one-off audit into a retainer.
4. **No commissions, ever.** No revenue from project partners, SAF suppliers, venues or offsite organisers, disclosed on the website. Every incumbent in destination-impact carbon takes a margin on the credits it recommends. Refusing that is a permanent moat and cannot be copied by anyone whose revenue depends on volume.

**Positioning sentence:** *We measure what your travel actually emits to an audit-ready standard, tell you how to emit less of it before you book, and connect you to named local restoration work at your destination — without letting you say a single thing about it that is now illegal.*

## 2. Regulatory Compliance & EU Framework Strategy (The Legal Shield)

### 2.1 CSRD alignment — how the deliverable actually lands

Your client is out of scope. Your client's **customer** is in scope. The deliverable therefore has to work as an input to somebody else's statutory report, which is a harder specification than a report of its own, because it must survive a third party's auditor.

**Where a Category 6 number travels.** Your client's Category 6 (their business travel) becomes part of their enterprise customer's **Category 1** (purchased goods and services) when that customer allocates supplier emissions to the services it bought. For that allocation to be usable, the receiving company's auditor needs four things beyond the tonnage: the **organisational and reporting boundary**, the **calculation method per activity type**, the **emission factor source and version**, and a **coverage ratio** against total travel spend. A bare tonnage figure is worthless to them. The four pieces of metadata are the product.

**Primary mapping — VSME.** Build every deliverable to the VSME module structure first. Rationale: it is the legal cap on requests to ≤1,000-employee undertakings, it is free and stable, and it is the only framework your client can point to when declining an over-broad questionnaire. Confirm the final adopted Delegated Act text and annex numbering before locking templates — the draft published 6 May 2026 is not the final instrument.

**Secondary mapping — ESRS E1-6.** Enterprise procurement teams will ask in ESRS vocabulary regardless of what their supplier is obliged to provide, so every deliverable carries an E1-6 crosswalk table. The revised ESRS (OJ 21 September 2026, applicable 1 January 2027) kept E1's full Scope 1/2/3 architecture while cutting mandatory datapoints elsewhere by over 60% — so the crosswalk you build now has unusual shelf life. Cover, at minimum: gross Scope 3 by category in tCO₂e; GWP100 on AR6 values; the calculation-method and factor-source disclosure; the share of primary versus secondary (estimated) data; and the base year with a stated recalculation policy.

**Three technical positions that separate you from everyone improvising this:**

1. **Radiative forcing must never enter the reported ESRS or VSME figure.** Aviation's non-CO₂ effects — contrails, NOₓ, water vapour — are not greenhouse gases accounted under GWP100. DESNZ 2026 supplies an RF multiplier of 1.7, and the GHG Protocol does not require its application. Blending it into the disclosed number inflates the figure against the framework's own basis and will be challenged. **Report the GWP100 figure as the disclosure number, and the RF-inclusive figure immediately beside it, separately labelled, as additional climate-impact information.** Both, always, clearly distinguished. This is a two-column habit that signals competence in about four seconds to anyone who knows the field, and it is the single most common error in travel-carbon reporting.
2. **Well-to-tank is disclosed as a separate line inside Category 6, never silently folded in.** DESNZ separates tank-to-wheel from WTT. WTT for third-party-operated travel belongs in Category 6, but the split must be visible or your client's auditor cannot reconcile against another supplier's differently-constructed number.
3. **Hotel nights belong in Category 6.** Commonly dropped, and for a four-night offsite they are material enough to change a per-attendee figure. Include them; cite the factor basis (CHSB/Greenview country values, or HCMI where the property publishes).

### 2.2 Anti-greenwashing — building on ECGT, not on a withdrawn proposal

**The instrument is Directive (EU) 2024/825 (ECGT), applicable since 27 September 2026.** It amends the Unfair Commercial Practices Directive by adding items to the Annex I blacklist — meaning these practices are unfair *per se*, with no case-by-case assessment of consumer detriment and no substantiation defence available.

What is now blacklisted, in the terms that matter here:

- Claiming a product or service has **neutral, reduced or positive environmental impact on the basis of greenhouse gas offsetting** — expressly *regardless of the quality or certification standard of the credits*. Gold Standard, Verra, ICVCM-labelled, CRCF-anticipating: all equally unavailable as the basis for a neutrality claim.
- **Generic environmental claims** without demonstrated recognised excellence — "eco-friendly", "green", "conscious", "sustainable travel programme", and — note carefully — **"regenerative"** used as an unqualified virtue word.
- Displaying a **sustainability label not based on a certification scheme or established by public authorities.** This is the clause that forbids you inventing a "CarbonMeter Verified" badge for clients, however commercially tempting.
- Unsubstantiated **future environmental performance** claims — a 2030 net-zero commitment without a published, funded, independently-milestoned implementation plan.

**The exception is the entire product strategy.** ECGT leaves intact claims grounded in **verified actual emission reductions within the value chain, documented with audit-ready Scope 1/2/3 data**. Read that as a product specification. It says: the only lawful route to a climate claim about an offsite runs through measured, in-value-chain reduction, evidenced to audit standard. Which is precisely and exactly what you sell — and it means the regulation has, in effect, banned your competitors' product and mandated yours.

#### The Portuguese trap

Portugal has **not transposed** ECGT. The 27 March 2026 deadline passed and the Commission opened infringement proceedings against Portugal among twenty Member States on 28 May 2026. ASAE is the designated enforcement authority.

Do not read this as breathing room. It is a trap, for four reasons, and explaining it correctly is a paid-consulting-grade insight you should give away in the first sales call:

1. **Your clients sell across the EU.** A directive binds through national transposition, so the practical constraint on a pan-European SaaS company is the *strictest transposed regime it touches*, not Portugal's. Germany, the Netherlands and Ireland are materially ahead.
2. **National case law already bites, without ECGT.** The Amsterdam District Court ruled against KLM's sustainability advertising in March 2024 — the defining precedent for travel-carbon claims specifically, and one your clients' communications teams should be handed on day one. The German Federal Court of Justice (BGH) held in June 2024 that "klimaneutral" requires clarification within the advertisement itself. Both were decided under pre-existing misleading-advertising law.
3. **Portugal already prohibits misleading commercial practices** under DL 57/2008 and the Código da Publicidade. ECGT's blacklist makes enforcement automatic; its absence does not make the underlying claim honest.
4. **Transposition is a question of when, not whether**, and a website makes claims continuously. A claim published today is still published the day the law lands.

#### The severance architecture

The compliance strategy is not disclaimer language. It is a structural separation maintained at five levels, so that no reader, auditor or regulator can construct an offsetting-based neutrality claim from your deliverables even if the client later wants one.

| Layer | Gross emissions | Local contribution |
|---|---|---|
| **Unit** | tCO₂e | € committed, hectares, trees, metres of seagrass, named project outputs |
| **Document location** | Sections 1–4 of the report | Section 6, after a hard divider and a standing non-offset statement |
| **Arithmetic** | Never netted, reduced or adjusted by the contribution | Never expressed in tonnes, never compared to the emissions figure |
| **Contract** | Measurement SOW | Separate Impact Programme SOW |
| **Money flow** | Client → LDA (advisory fee) | **Client → project directly. The LDA never receives, holds or forwards it.** |

**The tonnage rule, absolute:** local contributions are *never* quantified in tonnes of CO₂e, even where a project could support an estimate. The moment a contribution carries a tonnage, a reader can divide it into the emissions figure, and you have manufactured an implied offsetting claim you did not intend and cannot defend. Hectares, euros and project outputs cannot be divided into a carbon number. This single rule eliminates most of the legal risk in the business.

**Standing statement, verbatim in every report carrying a contribution section:**

> *These contributions are voluntary investments in named restoration work at the destination. They are not carbon credits, are not retired against any registry, and do not offset, neutralise or compensate any portion of the emissions reported in this document. The gross emissions figure in Section 2 is unaffected by them.*

#### The Claim Language Pack

A two-page annex, reviewed once by a Portuguese lawyer and reused across every client at zero marginal cost. Consistently the highest-perceived-value artefact you produce, because it is the only deliverable that tells a marketing team what to *do* on Monday.

**Prohibited — with reason, so the client internalises the rule rather than memorising a list:**

| Forbidden | Why |
|---|---|
| "Our offsite was carbon neutral" | ECGT Annex I — offset-based neutrality, blacklisted per se |
| "Climate-positive retreat" | Same, aggravated by an unsubstantiable net-positive assertion |
| "We offset our team travel" | Offsetting framing; also factually untrue under the contribution model |
| "Eco-friendly / sustainable / green offsite" | Generic environmental claim |
| "Regenerative travel programme" | Generic claim — the word is a conclusion, not a mechanism |
| "100% of our emissions are compensated" | Offsetting neutrality plus a quantified claim you cannot evidence |
| "CarbonMeter Verified" or any badge | Self-created sustainability label, blacklisted |
| "Net zero by 2030" *bare* | Unsubstantiated future performance absent a funded, milestoned plan |

**Permitted — method, boundary, factor version, coverage, every time:**

> "Our 2026 all-hands generated **192 tCO₂e** (GHG Protocol Scope 3 Category 6; distance-based method; DESNZ 2026 factors; GWP100/AR6; 96.4% of travel spend covered). Including aviation non-CO₂ effects at the DESNZ radiative-forcing multiplier of 1.7, the figure is **327 tCO₂e**."

> "Separately, and making no offsetting claim, we committed **€14,000** to the restoration of **6 hectares** of cork oak *montado* in Alentejo with [named partner]. We do not treat this contribution as reducing the emissions stated above."

> "We reduced per-attendee travel emissions **22%** against 2025 by relocating the all-hands from Lisbon to Barcelona and applying an economy-only policy on flights under eight hours. Both figures are recalculated on a consistent 2025 base year."

Note the mechanism of the third example: it names a *physical action*, quantifies the result, and states the comparison basis. That is the ECGT in-value-chain exception, used exactly as drafted.

**Contractual protection — put this in the MSA.** You cannot control a client's marketing team, but you can refuse to be cited by it:

> *Client shall not describe any emissions as offset, neutralised, compensated or balanced by reference to any contribution introduced under this Agreement. Client shall submit any public claim referencing Supplier, Supplier's methodology or Supplier's figures for review not less than five business days before publication. Supplier may withdraw permission to reference Supplier or its methodology on ten days' written notice where a claim is inconsistent with the Claim Language Pack.*

This clause does triple duty: it protects you from a client's greenwashing becoming your reputational problem, it creates a recurring review touchpoint that is a natural retainer hook, and it is itself a sales asset — it demonstrates that you take the rules more seriously than the client does.

### 2.3 Corporate structure boundaries — staying a consultancy

The LDA already exists, which removes incorporation cost and delay. The strategic work is defining the perimeter that keeps you a technical consultancy rather than a regulated financial intermediary. Register activity under business and technical consultancy CAE codes (70220 *outras actividades de consultoria para os negócios e a gestão*; 74900 *outras actividades de consultoria, científicas, técnicas e similares*) — confirm final coding with your TOC.

**Four bright lines. Treat as inviolable; each has an independent legal basis.**

**Line 1 — Never deal in EU ETS allowances or their derivatives.** EUAs are financial instruments under MiFID II Annex I Section C(11). Dealing on own account, receiving and transmitting orders, or arranging transactions in them is a regulated investment service requiring CMVM authorisation, and brings Market Abuse Regulation obligations with it. This line is not close to your business — but ETS aviation and CORSIA will come up in client conversations, and the correct posture is to explain the mechanism and never touch the instrument.

**Line 2 — Never take custody of contribution money.** The critical operational rule, with three independent justifications:
- **Payment services.** Collecting funds from a client for onward transfer to a third party can constitute money remittance under PSD2 and the Portuguese Regime Jurídico dos Serviços de Pagamento (DL 91/2018). The commercial-agent exclusion is narrow and does not obviously cover facilitating charitable contributions.
- **VAT.** Money routed through the LDA is presumptively consideration for a supply and therefore taxable turnover. A €40,000 pass-through becomes €40,000 of revenue subject to IVA analysis — contaminating your accounts and your margin optics for no benefit.
- **AML.** Handling third-party funds moves you toward obliged-entity territory under Lei 83/2017.

Implementation: the project invoices or receipts the client directly. You produce the due-diligence dossier, the recommendation and the stewardship reporting, and invoice *only* advisory fees. When a client's procurement team asks for a single consolidated invoice — they will — the answer is no, with the reason given, which lands as rigour rather than obstruction. If they cannot accept it, the project partner invoices them and you remain a disclosed, uncompensated introducer.

**Line 3 — Never describe yourself as a verifier, certifier, auditor or assurance provider.** Under CSRD, assurance is reserved to statutory auditors (ROC in Portugal) or accredited Independent Assurance Services Providers. CRCF certification bodies require accreditation under Implementing Regulation (EU) 2025/2358, with schemes recognised by the Commission from 7 May 2026. You hold none of these, and more fundamentally: **you cannot assure data you prepared.** Independence is not a formality, it is the whole content of assurance.

Turn this into a channel rather than accepting it as a limitation. The ROCs and assurance providers who *must* remain independent need well-prepared, evidence-backed Category 6 data from someone who is not them. You are structurally their preferred upstream, not their competitor. Two or three assurance-firm relationships will out-produce any outbound sequence you run — see Section 4.3.

**Line 4 — Never tokenise anything and never accept supplier commissions.** Tokenised credits risk classification as crypto-assets under MiCA (Regulation (EU) 2023/1114). Commissions from project partners, SAF suppliers, venues or offsite organisers destroy the independence proposition that is your only durable moat. Publish the no-commission policy on the website; it costs nothing and is unmatched by any incumbent whose economics depend on recommending volume.

### 2.4 Two positions worth holding early

**SAF and in-sector intervention is the lawful reduction lever.** Sustainable Aviation Fuel certificates under a book-and-claim framework (the SABA/RMI SAFc architecture) are an **in-value-chain intervention, not an offset** — placing them squarely inside the ECGT exception rather than inside the blacklist. ReFuelEU Aviation (Regulation (EU) 2023/2405) mandates 2% blending from 2025 rising to 6% by 2030, so supply and counterparties are becoming real. Advise on procurement specification, counterparty diligence, chain-of-custody documentation and claim wording. Take no commission and hold no inventory. As GHG Protocol market-based Scope 3 guidance finalises, confirm the treatment before advising on the accounting entry — the claim mechanics are still settling and you should say so to clients rather than overstate certainty.

**CRCF gives the local-project layer a 2028 destination.** The Carbon Removals and Carbon Farming Regulation (EU) 2024/3012 established EU certification for removals and carbon farming. The permanent-removals delegated act entered into force 7 May 2026; **carbon farming methodologies were expected summer 2026, with units from the 2027 harvest reaching buyers in H1 2028**. Two consequences: (a) CRCF-certified units are **not purchasable today**, which confirms the contribution model as the only honest structure available right now; and (b) Portuguese *montado*, soil-carbon and reforestation partners you cultivate through 2026–27 are exactly the counterparties who will generate CRCF units in 2028. Build the relationships now as contribution partnerships; convert to certified-unit advisory when the framework delivers. That is a two-year head start on a market with a legislated start date — the most defensible asset in this plan.

### 3.2 End-to-end workflow

**Stage 0 — Pre-sale: the Exposure Estimate (30 min, free).** Before any contract, produce a one-page Category 6 Exposure Estimate from public information alone: headcount, offsite cadence, team geography from LinkedIn, HQ or hub locations. Output a range (e.g. "280–380 tCO₂e, of which 60–70% from two events") plus the two or three levers visible from outside.

This single asset does four jobs: it is the lead magnet, the discovery call agenda, the scoping document, and the proof of competence. It takes 25 minutes once the model exists. **It is also the entire outbound strategy** — Section 4 sends the estimate, not a pitch.

**Stage 1 — Contract (day 0).** MSA + SOW + **Art. 28 GDPR Data Processing Agreement** + Data Handling Annex + Methodology Statement as a numbered schedule. Attaching the methodology *contractually* rather than describing it in marketing is what makes the engagement auditable; it also fixes scope against the mid-project expansion that kills fixed-fee consulting.

**Stage 2 — Kickoff (60 min).** Six decisions, minuted, and the minutes become Appendix A of the report:
1. **Single named Data Owner** with system access. No Data Owner, no start date — this is the most common cause of a three-week audit taking nine.
2. **Reporting boundary**: financial year, entity scope, subsidiaries, contractors.
3. **Consolidation approach**: financial control vs operational control. Recommend **operational control** for remote-first companies — it is more intuitive and better matches how they actually authorise travel.
4. **Base year** and the recalculation trigger threshold (recommend 5% of Category 6).
5. **De minimis rule**: up to 5% of Category 6 may be estimated rather than reconstructed, disclosed as such. Without this, the last 3% of rows consumes 30% of the hours.
6. **Data lane** (below).

**Stage 3 — Ingestion. Three lanes, chosen at kickoff.**

**Lane A — TMC / booking platform export (best case, ~20% of clients).** TravelPerk, Navan, Egencia. Export origin/destination IATA, cabin, date, traveller reference, routing. Supports full distance-based method with near-complete coverage. Two hours of work.

**Lane B — Expense system export (most common, ~60%).** Pleo, Payhawk, Spendesk, Expensify, Brex. You receive merchant, amount, date, employee — no itinerary. Two-pass reconstruction:
- *Pass 1 — classification.* Sort rows into air / rail / hotel / ground / catering / non-travel. Airline merchant strings are messy but finite; after four clients the lookup table covers most of what you will see, and this becomes the core of your accumulated advantage.
- *Pass 2 — hybrid method.* Where the itinerary is recoverable from the receipt or the booking reference, use distance-based. Where it is not, use spend-based (EXIOBASE or DESNZ spend factors). **Disclose the split as a headline metric**: "84% of Category 6 by value calculated distance-based, 16% spend-based."

That percentage is the honesty metric of the whole report. It is also the retainer hook — it *improves* year on year only if they change how they capture travel data, which is advice you give and they implement over quarters.

**Lane C — Attendee roster (offsites, and the fastest path to the tonnage).** A Google Form to attendees: home airport, routing, cabin, nights, ground transport. Twelve fields, 90 seconds per person, ~85% response rate with a nudge from the People team. Delivers ~95% accuracy on the single largest emissions source in the business, and requires no system access at all. **For a Tier 0 Snapshot this is usually the only lane needed** — which is why the Snapshot can be priced low and still earn its hours.

**Data minimisation — a genuine differentiator with People teams.** Flight manifests are personal data: travel patterns, and home airport is quasi-location data. You are a **processor**; the client is controller. Build pseudonymisation into ingestion: request **employee reference numbers, never names**; **home airport, never home address**. The client holds the mapping; you never receive it. Consequences: your DPA obligations shrink dramatically, no international transfer assessment is triggered, and the CPO's primary objection to handing a stranger their travel data disappears in the first call. Put this on the website.

**Stage 4 — Validation & QA.** The stage that distinguishes an audit from a spreadsheet:
- **Deduplicate** — same traveller, same day, same route (double-booked, rebooked, or expensed twice).
- **Coverage reconciliation** — sum the air spend in your dataset against the client's GL travel account. The resulting **coverage ratio** is the most important credibility artefact you produce: *"we account for 96.4% of your FY travel spend."* An auditor's first question is always "what's missing?" Answer it before it is asked.
- **Distance** — great-circle between airport pairs plus the **+9% uplift** for indirect routing, holding and taxi, consistent with DESNZ/GHG Protocol practice. State the uplift.
- **Haul-band and cabin mapping** — domestic / short-haul international / long-haul international per the DESNZ band definitions, with cabin class per segment. Cabin class is where the tonnage hides: a long-haul business seat is roughly **2.9×** economy. On a route where 15% fly business, cabin policy alone can move total event emissions 20%+.
- **Outlier review** — anything >3× the median trip gets looked at by a human. Usually a data error; occasionally the finding that carries the whole report.
- **Independent 10% re-run** — the second founder recalculates a 10% random sample from source without seeing the first result. Discrepancies >2% trigger a full re-run. This is your substitute for assurance you are not permitted to provide, it costs about 90 minutes, and it is describable to clients, which makes it worth more than it costs.

**Stage 5 — Calculation engine (Google Sheets, versioned, zero licence cost).** Six tabs:

| Tab | Function |
|---|---|
| `00_control` | Client, FY, boundary, consolidation approach, de minimis, factor set version, preparer, reviewer, sign-off dates |
| `10_ingest_raw` | Source data, unaltered. Never edited — provenance depends on it |
| `20_journeys` | One row per segment: traveller ref, O/D, haul band, cabin, distance, uplift, method, factor ID |
| `30_factors_DESNZ_2026` | **Locked and version-stamped.** Copied from the published workbook, never typed. Superseded sets retained, never overwritten |
| `40_results` | Category 6 by sub-source; GWP100 and RF-inclusive columns side by side; TTW/WTT split; distance-based vs spend-based share; coverage ratio |
| `90_audit_log` | Every assumption, exclusion, judgement call, and change with date and author |

Every row in `20_journeys` carries its own method, factor ID, distance basis and uplift flag. Row-level traceability is the difference between a number an auditor accepts and a number they reject, and retrofitting it is impossible — build it from client one.

Factor-set integrity is a hard operational rule: **one factor tab per published DESNZ release, copied not transcribed, locked, and never updated in place.** When DESNZ publishes the 2027 set, add a tab; do not edit the 2026 tab. Prior-year figures must remain reproducible on the factors in force when they were reported, or your base year — and every reduction claim resting on it — collapses.

**Stage 6 — The deliverable set (seven artefacts).**

| # | Artefact | Content | Audience |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | **Disclosure Table** | VSME-mapped, ESRS E1-6 crosswalked. Gross Cat 6 tCO₂e (GWP100/AR6), RF-inclusive figure separately, TTW/WTT split, sub-source breakdown, primary/secondary data share | Enterprise procurement, investors |
| **B** | **Methodology & Uncertainty Statement** (2–3pp) | GHG Protocol conformance, method hierarchy, factor source and version, coverage ratio, uncertainty band (±%), de minimis, exclusions, base year, recalculation policy | Their customer's auditor |
| **C** | **Evidence Binder** | Source exports, factor extracts, calculation workbook, audit log, kickoff minutes, reviewer sign-off | ROC / IASP |
| **D** | **Reduction Levers Memo** | Modelled, quantified scenarios — not recommendations. See 3.3 | CFO, Head of Ops |
| **E** | **Destination Impact Dossier** (Module A) | 2–3 vetted partners, diligence sheets, contribution structure, non-offset framing | People/Brand lead |
| **F** | **Claim Language Pack** | Permitted/prohibited sentences for website, careers page, RFP answers, investor updates | Marketing, Comms |
| **G** | **Live Dashboard** | Looker Studio on the Sheets backend, client-branded, read-only link | Everyone; renewal driver |

Artefacts B and C are what you are actually paid for and what nobody else at this price point produces. Artefact F is what the client *tells other people about*. Artefact G costs an hour and makes the retainer feel continuously alive rather than quarterly.

**Stage 7 — Readout and handover.** 60-minute readout to the People/Ops sponsor plus a 20-minute board-pack version they can present without you. Then set the recurrence: quarterly refresh and — the important one — **the offsite pre-mortem, calendared against their actual event planning cycle, six to ten weeks before the next event.**

### 3.3 The Reduction Levers Memo — where the advice earns its fee

Model the client's own data; never generalise. Levers ranked by observed impact on a distributed European/US workforce:

1. **Cabin class policy** — economy-only below 8 hours; premium economy rather than business above. Typically **15–25%** of total Category 6. The cheapest lever in existence: one line in a travel policy, zero capital, no cultural cost worth naming.
2. **Destination selection** — compute the *population-weighted travel centroid* of the actual workforce from home airports, then score candidate cities on (a) total passenger-km from real home airports, (b) direct-route availability, (c) rail substitutability for the European cohort, (d) venue capacity and cost, (e) presence of a credible local restoration partner. Moving an all-hands one or two cities typically swings **15–30%**. Clients pick destinations on venue Instagram and flight cost; nobody hands them this table, and it is the most-quoted slide you will produce.
3. **Trip duration and frequency** — the flight is fixed cost, so amortise it over more days of value. One five-day all-hands beats two three-day events on emissions; quantify the cultural trade rather than asserting it. Two regional offsites usually beat one global all-hands on tonnage but cost more in cohesion — **model it, present both, let them choose.** Your job is the number, not the decision.
4. **Rail substitution** — a hard rule for city pairs under 5–6 hours by rail. Limited in Iberia's international connections; strong for German, Benelux, French and Italian cohorts.
5. **Direct-routing rule** — connections add distance and a second takeoff/climb cycle, the most fuel-intensive phase.
6. **Booking lead time** — indirect but real: late booking forces worse routings and higher cabins.
7. **SAF allocation** (Module B) — the residual lever, applied *after* the six above. Ordering matters: recommending SAF before cabin policy signals you are selling a product rather than reducing emissions.

Present as modelled scenarios with stated assumptions, not as recommendations: *"Scenario 3: Barcelona + economy-only <8h + 5 days → 214 tCO₂e, −33% vs 2026 baseline, €18k lower travel spend."* Note how often the emissions-optimal option is also cheaper. On this client profile it usually is, and that is what gets the CFO to renew.

### 3.4 Destination project curation — the diligence framework

You are not buying credits, so ICVCM/Verra-style credit criteria do not apply directly — but the underlying integrity questions do, and applying them to contributions is what keeps the layer defensible. Every partner passes a written screen before it reaches a client. **Reject and record; a rejected-partner file is proof of rigour.**

| Criterion | Test | Fail condition |
|---|---|---|
| **Legal standing** | Registered entity, statutes, beneficial ownership, named accountable person | Informal collective with no legal personality or audited accounts |
| **Additionality** | Would this work happen anyway on public funding? What does marginal money buy? | Already fully funded by a Nature Restoration Regulation or Portugal 2030 line |
| **Permanence** | Land tenure horizon, legal protection, fire/drought/grazing risk and management | Restoration on land with <15 years' secured tenure |
| **Auditability** | Baseline documented, monitoring protocol, named verifier or research partner, published reports | "We plant trees" with no survival monitoring |
| **No double counting** | Not simultaneously generating credits sold to another party | Same hectares underpinning a voluntary credit issuance |
| **Co-benefits** | Biodiversity, water retention, fire resilience, local employment — evidenced | Monoculture eucalyptus or non-native plantation |
| **Safeguards** | Community consent, land-rights clarity, labour conditions | Contested tenure, no local consultation |
| **CRCF trajectory** | Could this qualify under CRCF carbon-farming methodologies from 2028? | No plausible pathway — acceptable, but note it |
| **Absorptive capacity** | Can they use €10–50k meaningfully and report on it? | Cannot account for restricted funds |

**Starter roster — Portugal/Iberia.** Diligence each before use; treat as a research agenda, not a vetted list:

- **Cork oak *montado* restoration**, Alentejo — agro-silvo-pastoral, exceptional biodiversity and fire-resilience co-benefits, strong CRCF carbon-farming trajectory. The flagship: culturally legible, visually distinctive, unmistakably Portuguese.
- **Post-fire native reforestation and fuel-break mosaics** — Pedrógão Grande (2017) and Serra da Estrela (2022) burn scars. Potential partners: Fundação Montis, ANP|WWF Portugal, Quercus, Plantar uma Árvore, and the relevant Comunidades Intermunicipais.
- **Seagrass and salt-marsh restoration (blue carbon)** — Ria Formosa, Sado estuary, Ria de Aveiro. CCMAR and University of Algarve as scientific anchors; Ocean Alive's Sado work with women fisherfolk carries an unusually strong co-benefit narrative.
- **Côa Valley rewilding** — Rewilding Portugal / Faia Brava. Large landscape scale, established monitoring.
- **Water-retention landscapes and anti-desertification**, interior Alentejo — Terra Sintrópica and similar. High climate-adaptation relevance in the fastest-drying part of Western Europe.
- **Laurissilva and Azorean endemic forest** restoration — Madeira and Azores. Relevant if an offsite lands there, which for this client segment it regularly does.

**Replication for other destinations.** Offsites concentrate in a predictable set of cities. Build the roster **two partners deep in the top six destinations only** — Lisbon, Porto, Barcelona, Madeira, Berlin, Amsterdam — and be honest with clients about coverage beyond it: a four-week diligence sprint at cost for a new destination, or relocate the offsite to a covered city. Never improvise a partner to win a deal; one badly-vetted partner ends the company's credibility permanently, and the whole proposition is that you do not do what everyone else does.

**Contribution structure — reiterating the hard rule.** Client contracts and pays the project **directly**. You produce diligence, design, claim framing and stewardship reporting, and invoice advisory fees only. Contributions are expressed in euros, hectares and project outputs — **never tonnes**. Stewardship reporting is photographic and narrative: hectares under restoration, survival rates, species recorded, people employed. This reports better internally than a tonnage ever did, and it cannot be divided into a carbon number by anyone.

### 4.1 Ideal Customer Profile

**Firmographic qualifiers (all must hold):**

| Attribute | Target |
|---|---|
| Headcount | 50–250 FTE (sweet spot 80–160) |
| Structure | Remote-first or remote-majority, no dominant HQ |
| Stage | Series A–C, or bootstrapped and profitable at >€5m ARR |
| Travel pattern | ≥2 company-funded gatherings per year, ≥1 involving intercontinental travel |
| Geography | EU-headquartered, or non-EU with EU entity and EU customers |

**Disqualifiers — leave immediately.** Under 40 FTE (no budget, no questionnaire pressure). Over 400 FTE (procurement cycle exceeds your cash runway). Already licensed Watershed/Plan A/Sweep (you become a feature request, not a vendor). Wants "carbon neutral" as the deliverable and will not be moved — **this is a hard disqualifier, not an objection to handle.** Selling them anything is selling them a liability that will eventually be attributed to you.

**Buying committee — sell to the owner of the offsite, not the owner of ESG:**

- **Economic buyer — Chief People Officer / Head of Remote.** Owns the offsite budget and the employer brand, and feels the pain directly. Their pain is *not* carbon; it is that they spent nine days last quarter answering a customer's sustainability questionnaire with numbers they did not trust. **Lead with the questionnaire, not the climate.**
- **Technical validator — Head of Finance / Sustainability Lead** (often a fractional or 20%-allocated role). Cares about method defensibility and whether it survives their customer's auditor. Artefacts B and C exist for this person.
- **Blocker — Legal / Comms.** Becomes an *advocate* the moment you show them the Claim Language Pack and the fact that ECGT went live on 27 September 2026. Convert them early; they accelerate deals more than the sponsor does.
- **Sponsor of last resort — CEO/founder.** At this company size, if a founder cares, it closes in two weeks. Worth a direct approach when the company has signed a public climate commitment — the founder personally signed it.

### 4.2 Trigger events — the whole basis of outbound

Do not send volume. Send relevance. Every contact requires a documented trigger, and the trigger goes in the first line of the message.

| # | Trigger | How to find it | Why it converts |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Offsite announced or location published** | LinkedIn posts, careers pages, remote handbooks, Instagram | You can price their exposure before they have booked. Highest-intent trigger available |
| 2 | **Public "carbon neutral / climate neutral" claim** | Site search for the phrases on remote-company domains | ECGT went live 27 Sept 2026. This is now a live liability and they do not know |
| 3 | **Sustainability/ESG role posted** | LinkedIn Jobs, Otta, Welcome to the Jungle | Budget exists, capacity does not. The best trigger of all seven |
| 4 | **Raised from an Article 8/9 SFDR fund or climate-committed VC** | Funding announcements, fund ESG pages, VC Climate Alliance / Leaders for Climate Action signatories | Portfolio reporting covenants are contractual and survived Omnibus untouched |
| 5 | **Won an enterprise logo that is CSRD-in-scope** | Case studies, "trusted by" logos, press | Supplier questionnaire is already in their inbox or will be within two quarters |
| 6 | **B Corp certification or recertification pending** | B Corp directory (pending status is public) | Recertification requires demonstrable climate action |
| 7 | **Signed SME Climate Hub / Race to Zero commitment** | Public signatory lists | Committed to halving by 2030 with no baseline. Naming this gently is devastating and welcome |

Triggers 2 and 7 combined are the most powerful list in this business: **companies that have publicly promised something they cannot currently evidence, in a week when the law on it just changed.** Build that list first.

### 4.3 Channels, in order of expected yield per hour

**1. Offsite organisers and venues — referral partnerships (highest leverage, most neglected).** Surf Office, Offsite/retreat platforms, boutique venue groups in Portugal and Spain, and independent offsite planners. They are in the room at the exact moment the destination decision is made — the moment your product is worth most and your diagnosis is still actionable. Offer: a free co-branded Exposure Estimate for any client shortlisting a destination, **no referral fee in either direction** (consistent with the no-commission policy, and it makes the partnership easier to agree, not harder). Target: four to six active partners by week 8. A single productive organiser relationship can out-produce your entire outbound sequence, because their clients arrive pre-qualified and pre-timed.

**2. Assurance firms and accountants.** Portuguese ROCs and mid-tier assurance providers who need prepared Category 6 data from someone structurally barred from assuring it (see 2.3, Line 3). You are their upstream, not their competitor. Two or three relationships compound quietly and indefinitely.

**3. Targeted outbound (LinkedIn + email).** The volume engine, detailed in 4.4. Realistic but not the top channel — deliberately third.

**4. Remote-work communities.** Running Remote, remote-work Slack and Discord communities, the Deel/Oyster/Remote.com partner ecosystems. Contribute the ECGT explainer, never pitch. Community selling is slow and compounding; start in week 1 so it is producing by month 4.

**5. Lisbon/Porto ecosystem and Web Summit (Lisbon, November).** You are local and Web Summit is on your doorstep. Work the periphery — side events, dinners, the offsite-planner and People-ops crowd — rather than buying anything. This is a concentrated, nearly free ICP densification event eight weeks from launch. Plan for it now.

**6. Content — exactly one asset, not a blog.** A single definitive public document: *"What ECGT means for your team offsite: the claims you can no longer make, and what to say instead."* Published with the permitted/prohibited table from 2.2. This is a genuine information gap, it is durable, it is the perfect outbound attachment, and it will be shared internally by exactly the Legal and Comms people who would otherwise block you. One excellent asset, updated quarterly, beats twenty posts.

### 4.4 Outbound mechanics — 100–150 contacts over 4–6 weeks

**Sourcing.** 25–30 companies/week, 4–5 contacts each. Free LinkedIn plus manual research is sufficient at this volume; take the Sales Navigator free trial in week 3 when you know what you are filtering for, and do not pay for it in month 1. Log every contact with its trigger — no trigger, no send. That rule is what makes a 100-contact campaign outperform a 2,000-contact one at this ACV.

**Deliverability under a €500 cap (non-negotiable; getting this wrong wastes the whole campaign):**
- Two mailboxes, both founders, on the real company domain. SPF, DKIM and DMARC configured before the first send.
- **Warm the domain for 14 days** before any cold volume: real conversations, partner outreach, community replies.
- **Maximum 20–25 cold sends per mailbox per day.** 50/day combined is ample for 150 contacts in three weeks.
- **Plain text. No tracking pixels, no link shorteners, no images.** Pixels hurt deliverability and, for a compliance vendor, are indefensible optics if noticed.
- One link maximum, and only from message two onward.

**Legal basis for B2B outbound in Portugal.** Portugal's ePrivacy transposition (Lei 41/2004, as amended) permits unsolicited commercial email to **corporate subscribers on an opt-out basis**, unlike natural persons who require prior consent. Process personal data in business contacts under GDPR Art. 6(1)(f) legitimate interests, with a documented Legitimate Interests Assessment on file, a clear opt-out in every message, honoured immediately and permanently, and no bulk scraping. Prefer named business addresses tied to a role you can evidence is relevant. **A compliance vendor that spams unlawfully is finished before it starts** — and the one-page LIA takes forty minutes and is itself a sales artefact when a prospect asks how you got their address.

**Sequence — four touches over 11 days, then stop.**

*Touch 1 (day 0) — the diagnosis, not the pitch. Subject: "Berlin offsite — rough carbon numbers"*

> Hi [Name],
>
> Saw you're taking the team to Berlin in March.
>
> Rough estimate from your team's public locations: ~55 travellers, ~85 tCO₂e, ~70% of it from the eight US-based people. Two or three of them flying business would be about a fifth of the event's total.
>
> I estimate travel emissions for remote-first companies — properly, to the standard your enterprise customers' auditors accept, not a spend-average.
>
> Want the one-page version for Berlin? No charge, takes me 25 minutes. If you're choosing between destinations I can price two or three and you'll see the spread before you pay the venue deposit.
>
> [Name]

Why this works: it leads with *their* specific number, demonstrates capability instead of asserting it, offers something genuinely free and immediately useful, and lands before the decision is locked. It never mentions carbon neutrality, CSRD, or sustainability as a value.

*Touch 2 (day 4) — the ECGT hook, for prospects with a public claim:* one short paragraph noting that offset-based neutrality claims became a blacklisted commercial practice across the EU on 27 September 2026 regardless of credit quality, that their [specific page] currently says [exact phrase], and a link to the explainer. **No pitch in this message at all.** Lead with the service, not the sale; this is the message that gets forwarded to Legal, and forwarded-to-Legal is how these deals actually start.

*Touch 3 (day 8) — social proof and specificity:* one sentence on a comparable company's result ("120-person company found 58% of their footprint in two events; moving one destination and changing cabin policy cut it 31% and saved €18k"), plus the single highest-value question you can ask: *"who owns the offsite budget over there?"*

*Touch 4 (day 11) — close the loop cleanly:* "I'll stop here — if offsite carbon becomes a question when the enterprise questionnaires land, I'm easy to find." No guilt, no fake deadline. This message produces replies for a year afterward, which is the actual reason to send it.

**Then stop.** Re-approach only on a new trigger. A trigger-based re-entry six months later converts far better than a fifth follow-up, and preserves the domain.

### 4.5 Funnel math — honest numbers

For 150 highly-researched contacts, every one carrying a documented trigger:

| Stage | Rate | Volume |
|---|---|---|

Anyone promising better than this from 150 cold contacts at this ACV is guessing.

### 5.4 Unit economics

Delivery hours fall steeply with templating. This learning curve *is* the business model:

| Product | 1st client | 5th | Steady state |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tier 0 Snapshot | 8h | 5h | **4h** |
| Tier 1 Audit | 45h | 24h | **18h** |
| Tier 2 Retainer (monthly) | 9h | 7h | **6h** |
| Module A Impact Programme | 22h | 16h | **14h** |

Marginal cash cost of delivery is effectively €0 — no licences, no data purchases, no subcontractors. So gross margin is an accounting fiction at MVP stage. **The real metric is € per founder-hour:**

| Product | Price | Steady hours | €/founder-hour |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tier 0 Snapshot** | €2,200 | 4h | **€550** |
| Tier 1 Audit | €7,500 | 18h | €417 |
| Assurance-Readiness Pack | €1,500 | 6h | €250 |
| SAF Advisory (Module B) | €2,500 | 10h | €250 |
| Tier 2 Retainer | €1,600/mo | 6h | €267 |
| Module A Impact Programme | €3,000 | 14h | €214 |

**Three findings that should change how you sell:**

1. **The Snapshot is your most profitable product per hour, not your loss leader.** It is priced as a wedge but earns €550/hour once templated. Sell many more of them than instinct suggests, and resist the urge to bundle them away free.
2. **Module A is your worst.** The diligence work is genuinely expensive and does not template well, because every destination is new. Either raise it to €4,000–4,500, or restrict it to the six destinations where your roster is already built and price new-destination diligence separately at cost. Do not let it become the product you are known for — it is the emotionally appealing part of the business and the economically weakest.
3. **Retainer €/hour is mediocre but its value is not in the hourly rate.** It is recurring, it compounds referrals, it makes the annual audit trivial to deliver (data is already clean), and it is what makes the company sellable. Accept the lower rate deliberately.

**Blended client value.** A full-stack client (Audit + Module A + 12-month retainer) = €7,500 + €3,000 + €19,200 = **€29,700 year one.** Realistically ~30% of audit clients take a retainer in year one, so model a blended ACV of **€11,000–13,000**.

### 5.5 Twelve-month model

**Base case:**

| Month | Activity | Revenue |
|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | Build engine, templates, roster, outbound campaign 1 | €0 |
| 3 | 1 Snapshot + 1 Audit deposit | €5,950 |
| 4 | 1 Snapshot + Audit balance + Audit deposit | €9,700 |
| 5 | 1 Audit + 1 Snapshot + Retainer #1 starts | €11,300 |
| 6 | 1 Audit + Retainer + Module A | €12,100 |
| 7 | 1 Audit + 2 Retainers + 1 Snapshot | €12,900 |
| 8 | 1 Audit + 2 Retainers | €10,700 |
| 9 | 1 Audit + 3 Retainers + Module A | €15,300 |
| 10 | 1 Audit + 3 Retainers + 1 Snapshot | €14,500 |
| 11 | 1 Audit + 4 Retainers | €13,900 |
| 12 | 1 Audit + 4 Retainers + Module A | €16,900 |
| **Year 1** | 9 Audits, 6 Snapshots, 4 Retainers, 3 Module A | **≈€123,000** |

Exit run rate ≈ €17k/month (≈ €200k annualised).

**Conservative case (60% of base, ≈€74,000):** 5 Audits, 4 Snapshots, 2 Retainers. Founder-salary breakeven slips to month 8–9 but still arrives inside year one. **Plan operations on the conservative case and staff ambition on the base case** — the difference between them is roughly four closed deals, which is well within the noise of a first-year funnel.

**Cash timing note.** The 50% deposit structure means month 3 produces ~€6k of cash against ~€700 of cost. The business is cash-positive from its first signed engagement and never needs external capital at this scale. That is the entire reason to accept the manual-delivery constraint.

### 5.6 The real constraint is capacity, not demand

At steady-state hours, two founders delivering ~80 productive hours/month each — with the other half of their time on sales, admin and research — cap out around **€22–28k/month, or €250–300k annualised**. The base case hits ~70% of that ceiling by month 12.

**So the strategic decision arrives at month 9, and it should be made deliberately rather than under pressure.** Two options:

**Option A — hire an analyst in Portugal.** €1,600–2,200/mo gross plus ~23.75% employer TSU ≈ €2,000–2,750/mo fully loaded. Breaks even at roughly 1.5 audits/month of incremental delivery, which is well inside the base case. Doubles delivery capacity in about six weeks of training, given templated processes.

**Option B — build software.** Automate ingestion, classification and report generation. Higher ceiling, but 4–6 months of founder time diverted from revenue, and it hard-codes methodology that is still changing (VSME final text pending, revised ESRS applying 1 Jan 2027, CRCF carbon farming methodologies landing, GHG Protocol market-based Scope 3 guidance unsettled).

**Recommendation: Option A, then Option B at month 18+.** Hire first. Build only once the methodology has stopped moving and you have thirty engagements' worth of merchant-classification data and factor logic worth encoding. Building the engine before the methodology settles means rebuilding it. Note also the **SIFIDE II** R&D tax credit as a later subsidy for Option B — a month-18 consideration, not a month-1 one.

**Concentration discipline:** no single client above **25% of trailing revenue by month 12**. At these deal sizes that is easy to comply with and easy to forget.

## 6. Risk Register & 90-Day Execution Plan

### 6.1 Risks, ranked by expected damage

| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A client publishes a banned claim citing your data** | Medium | **Severe** | Claim Language Pack in every engagement; MSA pre-publication review clause; right to withdraw attribution on 10 days' notice; disqualify prospects who insist on neutrality language |
| **Regulatory drift reduces perceived urgency further** | Medium | High | Already realised once via Omnibus. Mitigated structurally: sell commercial pressure (procurement, investors, talent), never legal fear. E1 survived the 60% datapoint cull — climate is the durable core |
| **Capacity ceiling at ~€250–300k** | **High** | Medium | Templating from client one; month-9 analyst hire; Snapshot-led mix for €/hour |
| **A badly-vetted local partner fails publicly** | Low | **Severe** | Written diligence screen on every partner, no exceptions; documented rejections; two-deep roster in six destinations only; never improvise a partner to win a deal |
| **Methodology error in a delivered report** | Medium | High | Independent 10% re-run; locked versioned factor tabs; uncertainty band disclosed; PI insurance bound before first engagement; liability capped at fees paid |
| **Key-person concentration (2 founders)** | High | Medium | Document everything in the workbook and audit log; both founders able to deliver every product; no client relationship held by only one |
| **Platform competitor moves down-market** | Medium | Medium | They cannot do done-for-you at €7,500, and cannot credibly refuse commissions. Stay narrow and human |
| **Portugal transposes ECGT with unexpected scope** | Medium | Low | Monitor monthly; you benefit from stricter enforcement either way |
| **GDPR incident with travel data** | Low | High | Pseudonymisation at ingest — employee refs and home airports only, no names, no addresses; Art. 28 DPA; no non-EU sub-processors |

### 6.2 90-day plan

**Weeks 1–2 — Foundation.** Register CAE codes with the TOC. Domain, Workspace, SPF/DKIM/DMARC, one-page site. Build the Sheets engine: six tabs, DESNZ 2026 factors copied and locked. Draft MSA, SOW, DPA, Data Handling Annex, Methodology Statement. Write the Legitimate Interests Assessment. **Begin domain warming.**

**Weeks 3–4 — Product and proof.** Build the Exposure Estimate template until it takes 25 minutes. Run the full Tier 1 workflow end-to-end on a **synthetic 120-person client** — this is the most valuable two days in the plan; it surfaces every gap before a paying client does. Draft the Claim Language Pack. Write the single content asset on ECGT. Begin partner diligence on the first three Portuguese projects. Source the first 60 triggered contacts.

**Weeks 5–8 — Market entry.** Launch outbound: 25–30 touch-1 sends/week. Approach 8–10 offsite organisers and venues for referral partnerships; target 4 active. Approach 3 ROCs/assurance firms. Publish the ECGT asset. Deliver Exposure Estimates on demand — target 2/week. **Target: 2 discovery calls/week, first Snapshot signed by week 7.** Bind PI insurance on the first signature. Book the €250 legal review of MSA and Claim Language Pack against first revenue.

**Weeks 9–12 — Delivery and conversion.** Deliver the first engagements, measuring hours ruthlessly against the 5.4 targets. Convert the first Snapshot to an Audit. Produce two anonymised case studies with real numbers. Complete diligence on partners 4–6. Second outbound campaign, retargeting the best non-responders on new triggers. Prepare for Web Summit (Lisbon, November) — side events and the People-ops periphery, not a stand. **Target by day 90: 2–4 paying clients, €6–15k collected, one retainer in negotiation, 4 referral partners live.**

### 6.3 Ten decisions to make before week 1

1. **Positioning vocabulary.** "Travel impact intelligence" or "Category 6 specialists"? Never "carbon consultancy" — and audit your own brand language against 2.2. **"Regenerative" is a generic environmental claim; name mechanisms instead.** Your own website is the first thing a prospect checks for hypocrisy.
2. **Brand name check.** "CarbonMeter" reads as a measurement instrument, which is right. Ensure no visual or verbal drift toward a certification mark — no seals, no badges, no "Verified", "Certified", "Neutral" or "Zero" anywhere, per the ECGT self-label prohibition.
3. **Founder split.** One owns delivery and methodology, the other sales and partnerships. Both must be able to deliver everything. Decide now, revisit at month 6.
4. **Confirm with the TOC:** CAE codes, *gerente* Segurança Social position, applicable IRC rate and *derrama*, AT-certified invoicing requirement, IVA reverse-charge mechanics for EU B2B (VIES validation and the *declaração recapitulativa*) and out-of-scope treatment for non-EU B2B.
5. **Insurance broker conversation this week**, binding deferred to first signature.
6. **Pick the six destination cities** for the two-deep partner roster. Lisbon and Porto are given; choose four.
7. **Set the disqualification rule in writing**, so it survives the first cash-hungry month: any prospect insisting on "carbon neutral" as a deliverable is refused.
8. **Write the no-commission policy and publish it** on the one-page site before the first outbound send. It is free, permanent, and unmatched.
9. **Decide the Snapshot-first rule** and hold it. Leading with the €7,500 audit is the most likely cause of a weak first campaign.
10. **Diarise the verify-before-use register** from Section 0 as recurring calendar items. A compliance advisory citing a withdrawn directive — as the original brief for this document did — is the fastest way to lose a client's trust, and the only defence is a calendar entry.
