# Rewrite Library

**Internal asset. The reason a remediation engagement takes two hours instead of eight.**

Each entry: the banned formulation, why it fails, and replacements at three tiers.

| Tier | Requires | Use when |
|---|---|---|
| **A — Withdraw** | Nothing | No data, no appetite, or the claim adds nothing commercially |
| **B — Action** | A true statement about something the client does | Most cases. Strongest option available without measurement |
| **C — Measured** | A quantified figure with method and boundary | The client has measured. **Strongest available claim in EU law** |

**Tier C is where the bridge sits.** Most clients cannot use it, because they have no number. Say so in the report as a fact, not a pitch: the strongest lawful claims require measurement, and they do not have it yet. That is an accurate observation about their position and it sells the carbon product without a single line of selling.

## 1. Neutrality claims

### "Carbon neutral" / "climate neutral" / "CO₂ neutral"

**Fails:** neutrality based on offsetting is blacklisted outright, regardless of credit quality or certification standard. No substantiation defence, no disclaimer cure.

- **A:** Delete. Where the sentence exists only to carry the claim, delete the sentence.
- **B:** *"We reduce the emissions of [activity] by [specific action]. We do not describe it as carbon neutral."*
- **C:** *"[Activity] emitted [X] tCO₂e in [year] ([method]; [factor set]; [coverage]%). We are reducing this by [action] and report progress annually."*

### "Net zero" used as an achieved state

**Fails:** same prohibition where it rests on offsetting; misleading where it rests on nothing.

- **A:** Delete.
- **B:** *"We are working to reduce our emissions. We have not reached net zero and do not claim to have."*
- **C:** *"We reduced absolute emissions [X]% between [base year] and [year]. We have not reached net zero."*

### "Climate positive" / "carbon negative" / "we give back more than we take"

**Fails:** the neutrality prohibition plus a net-positive assertion that is effectively unevidenceable.

- **A:** Delete. There is no safe version of this claim.
- **B:** *"We fund [specific restoration work] in addition to reducing our own emissions. We make no claim that this exceeds our impact."*

### "Plastic neutral" / "water neutral" / "plastic positive"

**Fails:** same structure — a neutrality claim achieved by paying a third party. Commonly assumed to be outside the carbon rules. It is not.

- **A:** Delete.
- **B:** *"We fund [collection / restoration programme] with [partner]. This is separate from our own [plastic / water] use, which we are reducing by [action]."*

## 2. Offsetting claims

### "We offset our emissions" / "100% offset" / "offset at checkout"

**Fails:** offsetting framing in support of an impact claim. Note that offering customers an offset purchase is not itself prohibited — **describing the result as neutral or reduced is.**

- **A:** Remove the claim; where an offset purchase option exists, describe it factually without an impact claim: *"You can contribute €[X] to [named project]. This does not neutralise the emissions of your order."*
- **B:** *"We fund [named project]. Separately, we have reduced [activity] emissions by [action]."*

### "Carbon compensated" / "climate compensated" / "emissions balanced"

**Fails:** the same prohibition in plainer words. Frequently believed to be a safe synonym. It is not.

- **A:** Delete.
- **B:** As above — separate the contribution from any impact claim.

### "Carbon neutral shipping" / "carbon neutral delivery"

**Fails:** product-and-service-level neutrality by offsetting. **The single most common live violation in EU ecommerce.**

- **A:** *"Standard delivery."* Delete the claim entirely.
- **B:** *"We ship [X]% of orders by [rail / sea / consolidated freight] and have reduced packaging weight by [Y]%."*
- **C:** *"Average delivery emissions per order: [X] kgCO₂e ([method]). Down [Y]% since [year] through [action]."*

### "We plant a tree for every [order / flight / signup]"

**Fails:** implies offsetting and a permanence the client cannot evidence. Trees planted is an input, not an outcome — survival rate is the outcome, and almost nobody tracks it.

- **A:** Delete.
- **B:** *"We fund [partner] to restore [X] hectares of [ecosystem] in [location]. [Y]% of plantings surveyed alive after [Z] months."* Only where the partner actually reports survival.

## 3. Generic environmental claims

### "Eco-friendly" / "environmentally friendly" / "green" / "planet-friendly"

**Fails:** generic environmental claim without demonstrated recognised excellence.

- **A:** Delete the adjective. In most cases the sentence reads better: *"our packaging"* rather than *"our eco-friendly packaging"*.
- **B:** Replace with the mechanism: *"packaging made from [X]% post-consumer recycled cardboard"*, *"servers running on [supplier]'s [tariff]"*.

### "Sustainable" / "sustainability-first" / "sustainable [product]"

**Fails:** generic claim when applied to a product or company as a virtue. Note: *"our sustainability report"*, *"our sustainability team"* and *"sustainability regulations"* are descriptive uses and are fine — the failure is using it as a claim of merit.

- **A:** Delete or rename to something descriptive: *"our environmental reporting"*.
- **B:** Name the mechanism instead.

### "Regenerative" / "restorative" / "nature-positive"

**Fails:** generic claim. The word is a conclusion, not a mechanism.

- **B:** *"We fund cork oak montado restoration in Alentejo with [partner]"* — name the ecosystem, the location and the partner.

### "Conscious" / "mindful" / "responsible" / "ethical" / "low impact" / "zero impact"

**Fails:** generic, and vaguer than the terms above. "Zero impact" is additionally an absolute claim that is false on its face.

- **A:** Delete.
- **B:** State what was done.

## 4. Labels, badges and seals

### Self-designed badge or seal

**Fails:** displaying a sustainability label not based on a certification scheme or established by a public authority.

- **A:** Remove the graphic. Replace with plain text stating the underlying fact, if there is one.
- **Never:** redesign it to look less official. The prohibition is on the label, not its styling.

### Vendor-supplied badge ("Carbon Neutral Certified by [offset provider]")

**Fails:** both prohibitions at once — offsetting neutrality and a label outside a recognised scheme.

- **A:** Remove. **Tell the client plainly that passing on a supplier's neutrality claim makes it their own claim**, and that "our vendor certified it" is not a substantiation.

### Third-party scheme logo

**Not automatically a failure.** Verify: the scheme exists, it is a certification scheme or public-authority label, the client holds current certification, and the logo is used within the scheme's own terms. Where any of those fails, treat as P1.

## 5. Forward-looking claims

### "Net zero by 2030" / "carbon neutral by 2040" (bare)

**Fails:** unsubstantiated future environmental performance.

- **A:** Delete the target, publish the trajectory: *"We reduced [activity] emissions [X]% between [base year] and [year]. We have not set a dated reduction target and will publish one when we can commit to milestones and report against them."*

  This is a genuinely strong statement and safer than any aspirational date. Clients resist it for about ten minutes, then prefer it.
- **C:** Keep the target only where all five tests pass — base year and boundary, published milestone plan, allocated resource, annual public progress reporting, third-party verification where feasible. Where four of five pass, fix the fifth rather than delete.

### "Committed to net zero" / "on a journey to net zero"

**Fails:** same, with vaguer wording that provides no additional protection.

- **A:** Delete.

## 6. Implied and visual claims

### "Eco" / "sustainable choice" product filter or badge

**Fails:** a generic environmental claim expressed through UI, with no defined criteria.

- **A:** Remove the filter, or rename to the actual attribute: *"Made with recycled content"*, *"Refillable"*, *"Ships without plastic"*.
- **B:** Publish the criteria and rename to the criterion.

### Nature imagery and green colour coding adjacent to claims

**Fails:** overall impression may carry a claim the words do not support.

- **A:** Decouple the imagery from the claim area, or support it with a specific factual statement.

## 7. Comparative claims

### "Greener than" / "X% more sustainable than"

**Fails:** generic wording, plus a comparison with no stated basis.

- **B:** *"[Product] uses [X]% less [material] than [named comparator], measured [basis]."* Requires equivalent basis, same boundary, same method — and where a figure is involved, an unrestated change of method or factor set invalidates it.

## The three sentences to hand every client

However bad the scan, these always work and require no data:

1. *"We do not describe [activity] as carbon neutral."*
2. *"We fund [named work] with [named partner] in [location]. We make no claim that this offsets our emissions."*
3. *"We have not measured [X] yet. When we do, we will publish the figure and its method."*

Sentence 3 is the most underused sentence in EU sustainability communication. **Nothing in the law requires a company to make an environmental claim** — only to be accurate about the ones it chooses to make. Silence is a complete defence and costs nothing.
