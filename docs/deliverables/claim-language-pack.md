This is a practical drafting standard built on the EU consumer-protection rules governing environmental claims, applied to your measured emissions data. It is designed so that a marketing or people team can write confidently without consulting anyone.

**It is not legal advice, and CarbonMeter is not a law firm.** We are the preparer of your emissions data — a technical consultancy. ## Part 1 — The five rules

If you remember nothing else, remember these. Each maps to a specific legal prohibition explained in Part 8.

### 1. Never net.

Your gross emissions figure stands alone. Nothing reduces it except actually emitting less. Not a contribution, not a purchase, not a partnership, not a certificate. Never write a sentence in which a payment makes a number smaller.

### 2. Never put a tonnage on a contribution.

Money you give to restoration work is expressed in **euros, hectares, metres, species or people employed — never in tCO₂e.** The moment a contribution carries a tonnage, any reader can divide it into your emissions figure, and you have made an offsetting claim you did not intend and cannot defend. Euros and hectares cannot be divided into a carbon number. This one rule removes most of your legal risk.

### 3. Claim an action, not a state.

**Banned:** "we are carbon neutral" (a state). **Permitted:** "we moved the all-hands to Barcelona and cut per-attendee travel emissions 22%" (an action with a measured result).

This is the most useful sentence in this document. EU law has closed off claims about environmental *status* achieved by paying someone, and left open claims about *reductions you actually achieved in your own value chain, evidenced to audit standard*. Write about what you did and what it measurably changed. That route is fully open and nobody can challenge it.

### 4. Never state a figure without the four.

Every emissions number you publish carries: **method**, **boundary**, **factor version**, **coverage**. Part 3.1 gives you the pattern. A figure without these is not a disclosure, it is a marketing claim — and a marketing claim is what gets challenged.

### 5. Never invent a badge.

No seals, no marks, no "Climate Verified", no self-designed logo implying environmental approval. Displaying a sustainability label that is not based on a third-party certification scheme or established by a public authority is prohibited outright. This includes badges you design for yourself, and badges a supplier designs for you. **CarbonMeter will never issue you one, and you should decline any vendor who offers one.**

## Part 2 — Prohibited claims

These are prohibited **per se**: there is no substantiation defence, no case-by-case assessment, and no disclaimer that rescues them. A footnote does not fix a banned headline.

| Do not say | Why |
|---|---|
| "Carbon neutral [offsite / event / company / travel]" | Neutrality claim based on offsetting — blacklisted outright, **regardless of the quality or certification standard of the credits** |
| "Climate neutral", "CO₂ neutral", "net zero offsite" | Same prohibition, different wording |
| "Climate positive", "carbon negative", "we give back more than we take" | Same, aggravated by a net-positive assertion you cannot evidence |
| "We offset our team travel" | Offsetting framing — and factually untrue under your contribution model |
| "Our emissions are compensated / balanced / cancelled out" | Same prohibition in plainer words |
| "Eco-friendly", "green", "sustainable", "conscious", "planet-friendly" offsite/travel | Generic environmental claim without demonstrated recognised excellence |
| "Regenerative travel" / "regenerative offsite" *(unqualified)* | Generic claim. "Regenerative" is a conclusion, not a mechanism. Name the mechanism instead — see 3.3 |
| "Responsible / mindful / low-impact travel programme" | Generic claim, vaguer than the ones above |
| Any badge, seal or mark of your own design | Self-created sustainability label |
| "Net zero by 2030" *(bare)* | Unsubstantiated future performance — see 4.3 for what makes this sayable |
| "Carbon-neutral certified by [vendor]" | Both prohibitions at once: offsetting neutrality **and** a label not backed by a recognised scheme |
| "Sustainably powered flights" / "green flying" | Generic claim about a service you do not control |
| "We plant a tree for every flight" *(as an impact claim)* | Implies offsetting; tree counts are inputs, not outcomes, and imply permanence you cannot evidence |

**Also prohibited in substance, though it looks like design rather than copy:** claims made *visually*. A page headed "Our offsite" over forest imagery, green colour-coding and a leaf motif can constitute an implied generic environmental claim even with no words at all. Environmental claims are assessed on the overall impression created by any means, including imagery, colour and layout. Brief your designers, not only your writers.

### The consequences, stated plainly

For widespread cross-border infringements, Member States must make available maximum fines of **at least 4% of annual turnover**, or at least €2 million where turnover figures are unavailable. Penalties are set against criteria that expressly include the **nature, gravity and duration** of the infringement.

**Read "duration" carefully: how long a claim stays live is itself an aggravating factor.** This is why Part 7 is urgent rather than cosmetic. A banned claim that sat on your homepage for eighteen months is a materially worse position than the same claim removed within a fortnight.

## Part 3 — Approved sentence library

Copy these verbatim, substituting only the bracketed fields from your [[FY]] report. **Verbatim use requires no further approval.**

### 3.1 The core disclosure sentence

> In [[FY]], [[CLIENT_NAME]]'s business travel generated **[[GROSS_TCO2E]] tCO₂e** (GHG Protocol Scope 3 Category 6; distance-based method; [[FACTOR_SET]] emission factors; GWP100 on IPCC AR6 values; [[COVERAGE_PCT]]% of business travel spend covered). Including aviation non-CO₂ effects at the [[FACTOR_SET]] radiative-forcing multiplier of [[RF_MULTIPLIER]], the figure is **[[RF_TCO2E]] tCO₂e**.

**Never drop the parenthesis.** It is what converts a marketing number into a disclosure. It is also what your enterprise customers' auditors need, so it does commercial work as well as legal work.

**Short form** — where space genuinely does not permit, and only with the full version one click away:

> Business travel, [[FY]]: **[[GROSS_TCO2E]] tCO₂e** (Scope 3 Cat. 6, distance-based, [[FACTOR_SET]], [[COVERAGE_PCT]]% coverage). [Full methodology →]

### 3.2 Event-level figure

> Our [[FY]] all-hands in [[CITY]] brought [[HEADCOUNT]] people together and generated **[[EVENT_TCO2E]] tCO₂e** — [[EVENT_PER_HEAD]] tCO₂e per attendee — of which [[AIR_PCT]]% was air travel. [[LONGHAUL_PCT]]% of the total came from the [[LONGHAUL_COUNT]] colleagues travelling intercontinentally.

Publishing the concentration is *good* practice: it evidences that you understand your own footprint rather than reciting a total, and it sets up the reduction claim in 3.3.

### 3.3 Reduction claims — your strongest available ground

The pattern is: **physical action → measured result → comparison basis.**

> We reduced per-attendee travel emissions **[[PCT]]%** against [[BASE_YEAR]] by relocating the all-hands from [[OLD_CITY]] to [[NEW_CITY]] and applying an economy-class policy on flights under eight hours. Both years are calculated on a consistent boundary and recalculated on [[FACTOR_SET]] factors.

> Moving our [[FY]] regional offsite from [[OLD_CITY]] to [[NEW_CITY]] shortened total distance travelled by [[PKM_PCT]]% and reduced event emissions from [[OLD_TCO2E]] to [[NEW_TCO2E]] tCO₂e.

> [[PCT]]% of our European team travelled to the [[FY]] offsite by rail rather than air, avoiding an estimated [[AVOIDED_TCO2E]] tCO₂e against the equivalent flights.

> We extended our all-hands from [[OLD_DAYS]] to [[NEW_DAYS]] days and reduced the number of company-wide gatherings from [[OLD_COUNT]] to [[NEW_COUNT]] per year, cutting annual business travel emissions [[PCT]]%.

Note what these have in common: each names something you *did*, quantifies what changed, and states the comparison basis. That is precisely the ground EU law leaves open, and it cannot be attacked.

### 3.4 Contribution claims — the severance wording

Use only in a section clearly separated from your emissions figures, and **always with the standing statement below.**

> Separately from our emissions accounting, we committed **€[[AMOUNT]]** to [[PROJECT_DESCRIPTION]] with [[PARTNER_NAME]] in [[LOCATION]]. This supported **[[OUTPUT_1]]** and **[[OUTPUT_2]]**.

Examples of correctly-formed outputs: *"the restoration of 6 hectares of cork oak montado"*; *"1,400 metres of seagrass meadow under active restoration"*; *"three seasonal positions for local fisherfolk"*. Examples of incorrectly-formed outputs: anything containing "tonnes", "offset", "equivalent to", or "balancing".

**Standing statement — reproduce verbatim, in the same visual block, at equal prominence:**

> *These contributions are voluntary investments in named restoration work. They are not carbon credits, are not retired against any registry, and do not offset, neutralise or compensate any portion of the emissions reported above. Our reported emissions figure is unaffected by them.*

This statement is not defensive boilerplate. It is the structural feature that makes the contribution section lawful, and removing it to save space removes the protection.

### 3.5 Customer questionnaires, RFPs and investor updates

> **Business travel emissions ([[FY]]):** [[GROSS_TCO2E]] tCO₂e, GHG Protocol Scope 3 Category 6. Method: distance-based for [[DISTANCE_PCT]]% of spend, spend-based for the remainder. Factors: [[FACTOR_SET]], GWP100/AR6. Coverage: [[COVERAGE_PCT]]% of business travel spend reconciled to the general ledger. Boundary: [[BOUNDARY]], operational control. Base year: [[BASE_YEAR]]. Methodology statement and evidence binder available on request.

> We are not within the scope of the Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive. We report business travel emissions voluntarily, prepared to the GHG Protocol Corporate Value Chain (Scope 3) Standard and mapped to the VSME standard, so that customers in scope of CSRD can use our figures in their own Category 1 reporting.

That second paragraph is worth including unprompted. Stating your non-scope openly, then exceeding what is asked, reads as competence. Claiming a compliance obligation you do not have reads as either confusion or marketing, and a procurement team will notice.

### 3.6 Careers page and employer brand

> We are a distributed company and we fly people to meet each other, which has a real climate cost. In [[FY]] that cost was **[[GROSS_TCO2E]] tCO₂e**, [[CAT6_PCT]]% of our total measured footprint. We publish the number, we measure it properly, and we are working to reduce it — [[REDUCTION_ACTION]]. We do not describe our travel as carbon neutral, because under EU law and in plain fact, it is not.

The last clause converts a compliance constraint into a credibility asset. Candidates in this market are unusually literate about greenwashing, and the honest version of this sentence outperforms the aspirational version.

### 3.7 Social and employee posts

Employee amplification you encourage can be treated as your own commercial practice. Give your team approved copy rather than a prohibition — people post anyway, and a ban produces silence from the compliant and risk from everyone else.

**Approved for employees to copy:**

> Great week with the team in [[CITY]]. Our travel to get there came to [[EVENT_TCO2E]] tCO₂e — we measure it and publish it here: [link]

**Add to your social media guidelines:** *Do not describe company events or travel as carbon neutral, climate neutral, offset, green, eco-friendly or sustainable. If you want to mention our climate work, link to [page] or use the approved wording above.*

### 3.8 Event signage and on-site materials

Frequently forgotten and unusually exposed, because a banner is photographed and outlives the event. Same rules apply: no "carbon neutral event" signage, no leaf-and-globe iconography implying neutrality.

**Approved:** *"Getting us all here emitted [[EVENT_TCO2E]] tCO₂e. Here's what we're doing about it: [QR code]"*

## Part 4 — The four claim types and what each requires

Every environmental claim you make is one of four types. Each has a different evidence requirement, and the failures are type-specific.

### 4.1 Absolute figure claims — "we emitted X"

**Requires:** method, boundary, factor set and version, coverage ratio, reporting period. All four are in your [[FY]] Methodology Statement; the sentence pattern is 3.1.

**Where these fail:** a figure quoted without its basis, or quoted from a stale report. Retire superseded figures actively — an old number left on a live page is a wrong claim, not a historical record, unless it is explicitly dated and labelled as such.

### 4.2 Comparative claims — "X% lower than last year"

**The most common source of accidental misstatement, because the failure is arithmetic rather than verbal.** A comparison is only valid where both years share:

- the same organisational boundary (no acquisitions, entity changes or headcount-scope changes unaccounted for)
- the same calculation method (a shift from spend-based to distance-based will change the number on its own)
- the same emission factor set, **or** an explicit recalculation of the earlier year onto the current set

Emission factors are revised annually. **If your factor set changed between the two years and you did not restate the earlier one, your comparison is invalid** — even though both figures are individually correct. This is the single most likely way [[CLIENT_NAME]] makes a misleading claim in complete good faith.

**Rule:** before publishing any year-on-year comparison, confirm with CarbonMeter that the base year has been recalculated on the current factor set. This is a two-line email and it is covered by your engagement.

**Also required:** state the comparison basis in the claim itself — "against a recalculated [[BASE_YEAR]] baseline on a consistent boundary."

**The intensity trap.** Per-employee or per-revenue figures can fall while absolute emissions rise, because you grew. Publishing only the intensity metric in that situation is misleading by omission. **Where absolute emissions increased, say so in the same breath as any intensity improvement:**

> Our travel emissions per employee fell [[INTENSITY_PCT]]% in [[FY]], while absolute travel emissions rose [[ABSOLUTE_PCT]]% as headcount grew from [[OLD_FTE]] to [[NEW_FTE]].

That sentence is harder to write and much harder to attack. Publishing only the first half is the kind of claim that gets escalated by a competitor.

### 4.3 Forward-looking claims — "we will reduce X by 2030"

The weakest ground in this document, and the source of a great deal of enforcement risk across the market. Unsubstantiated future environmental performance claims are expressly targeted.

**A target is publishable only if all five hold:**

1. A stated **base year** and **boundary** (a target without a base year means nothing)
2. A **published implementation plan** with dated interim milestones — not a direction of travel
3. **Allocated budget or committed resource** for the actions in that plan
4. **Regular public progress reporting** against the milestones, at least annually
5. Milestones subject to **third-party verification** where feasible

Where you cannot meet all five, publish the trajectory without the pledge:

> We reduced business travel emissions [[PCT]]% between [[BASE_YEAR]] and [[FY]]. We have not set a dated reduction target and will publish one when we can commit to milestones and report against them.

That is a genuinely strong statement and far safer than an aspirational net-zero date. If you want a defensible target, a validated science-based target is the established route; treat that as a separate project with its own timeline, not a copy decision.

### 4.4 Contribution claims — "we funded X"

**Requires:** named partner, named location, described mechanism, euro amount, physical outputs, and the standing statement from 3.4. **Must not contain:** any tonnage, any offsetting vocabulary, any implied relationship to your emissions figure, any placement that invites the reader to net one against the other.

**Placement rule:** contributions appear *after* emissions in reading order, in a visually distinct block, under a heading that does not imply compensation. Acceptable heading: "Restoration work we fund." Unacceptable: "How we address our footprint", "Balancing our impact", "Our climate contribution" — each of which implies the netting that Rule 1 forbids.

Run this on any copy that deviates from Part 3. Ten questions, three minutes. **If any answer is wrong, do not publish — rewrite or escalate.**

| # | Question | Required answer |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Does any sentence claim a neutral, net-zero, positive or compensated state? | **No** |
| 2 | Is any contribution, payment or partnership presented as reducing an emissions figure? | **No** |
| 3 | Does any contribution appear in tonnes of CO₂e? | **No** |
| 4 | Does every emissions figure carry method, boundary, factor version and coverage? | **Yes** |
| 5 | Is every comparative claim on a consistent boundary with a recalculated base year? | **Yes** |
| 6 | Where intensity improved but absolute emissions rose, are both disclosed? | **Yes** |
| 7 | Does every forward-looking claim meet all five tests in 4.3? | **Yes** |

## Part 6 — Escalate to counsel

Stop and obtain legal advice before publishing when any of the following applies. This list is short on purpose; treat it as binding.

- A claim you want to make is not in Part 3 and you cannot get all ten checklist answers right
- Any paid advertising or sponsored content containing an environmental claim
- Any claim comparing [[CLIENT_NAME]] to a named competitor
- Any use of a third-party certification, label or scheme logo — including checking that you are entitled to display it
- Any claim in a regulated context: a prospectus, a fund document, a tender to a public authority
- A customer, investor or competitor challenges a claim, or asks you to substantiate one in writing
- Any contact from a consumer-protection authority (in Portugal, **ASAE**), an advertising regulator, or a self-regulatory body
- A supplier offers you a "carbon neutral" product, badge or certificate, and you are considering passing that claim on to your own audience

The last item is the one most often missed. **Reselling a supplier's neutrality claim makes it your claim.** You cannot inherit a defence from a vendor's marketing, and "our supplier told us it was certified" is not a substantiation.

## Part 7 — Remediating claims already live

Start this week. Duration is an explicit penalty criterion, so time to remediation is itself part of your legal position.

**Step 1 — Inventory (half a day).** Search every property you control for: *carbon neutral, climate neutral, CO2 neutral, net zero, offset, compensated, carbon positive, climate positive, eco-friendly, green, sustainable, regenerative, planet*. Cover the website, careers pages, blog archive, product pages, pitch decks in circulation, PDFs, email footers, social bios, pinned posts, event pages and app store listings. PDFs and old blog posts are the usual offenders because nobody owns them.

**Step 2 — Triage.**

| Priority | What | Action | Deadline |
|---|---|---|---|
| **P0** | Live neutrality or offsetting claims on consumer-reachable pages; any paid ad | Rewrite or remove | **48 hours** |
| **P1** | Generic claims ("eco-friendly", "sustainable travel"); self-made badges | Rewrite using Part 3 | 2 weeks |
| **P2** | Unsubstantiated forward-looking claims and targets | Rewrite per 4.3 | 30 days |
| **P3** | Archived posts, superseded PDFs, old decks | Date-label, rewrite or withdraw | 60 days |

**Step 3 — Record what you did.** For each change: the original wording, the date it was first published, the date changed, the new wording, and who approved it. Keep this alongside the claim register.

Recording the remediation is as important as doing it. Prompt, documented, self-initiated correction is a materially better position than a change a regulator cannot date — and if you cannot show *when* you fixed something, you cannot rebut an assumption that it was live throughout.

**Step 4 — Deal with what you do not control.** Press releases on third-party wires, partner websites, podcast descriptions, conference bios, cached and archived copies. Request corrections in writing and keep the correspondence; you cannot remove an internet archive, but you can evidence that you asked. Where a partner refuses, note it in the register.

**Step 5 — Close the gate.** Add the Part 5 checklist to your content workflow and the social guidance from 3.7 to your handbook, so remediation is a one-time exercise rather than an annual one.

## Part 8 — The legal basis, in brief

For readers who want to know why, and for your legal counsel's orientation. Current as at **[[ISSUE_DATE]]**.

**The operative instrument is Directive (EU) 2024/825 (ECGT / "Empowering Consumers for the Green Transition"), applicable across the EU from 27 September 2026.** It amends the Unfair Commercial Practices Directive (2005/29/EC), adding items to the Annex I blacklist. Blacklisted practices are unfair *per se*: no assessment of consumer detriment is required and no substantiation defence is available. The prohibitions relevant to you are (i) claiming neutral, reduced or positive environmental impact **on the basis of greenhouse gas offsetting**, expressly regardless of credit quality or certification standard; (ii) generic environmental claims without demonstrated recognised excellence; (iii) displaying a sustainability label not based on a certification scheme or established by a public authority; and (iv) unsubstantiated claims about future environmental performance.

**What was *not* the basis, contrary to widespread belief:** the separate **Green Claims Directive** proposal. The Commission announced its intention to withdraw it in June 2025 and the file has not proceeded. Advisers and vendors still citing it as a forthcoming obligation are working from outdated material. Nothing in your compliance position depends on it.

**Penalties.** Set by Member States. For widespread cross-border infringements, maximum available fines must be at least **4% of annual turnover**, or at least €2 million where turnover information is unavailable. Penalty criteria expressly include the nature, gravity and **duration** of the infringement.

**Evidence.** Traders must be able to furnish evidence of the accuracy of factual claims made in a commercial practice. Hence Part 5's claim register.

**Portugal specifically.** Portugal had not transposed the ECGT Directive as at [[ISSUE_DATE]]; the Commission opened infringement proceedings against Portugal, among twenty Member States, on 28 May 2026. **This is not shelter, for four reasons:** (i) the Directive applies across the Union and your audience is not confined to Portugal — the practical constraint is the strictest transposed regime you reach; (ii) Portuguese law already prohibits misleading commercial practices under DL 57/2008 and the Código da Publicidade; (iii) national courts have already decided cases against travel-sector climate claims under pre-existing misleading-advertising law, including the Amsterdam District Court's March 2024 ruling on KLM's sustainability advertising and the German Federal Court of Justice's June 2024 ruling that *"klimaneutral"* requires clarification within the advertisement itself; and (iv) transposition is a matter of when, not whether, while a website claims continuously. ASAE is the designated Portuguese enforcement authority.

**B2B versus B2C — the honest position.** The UCPD, and therefore the ECGT amendments, govern **business-to-consumer** practices; pure B2B practices fall outside its scope at EU level. It would be overstating the law to tell you the blacklist directly governs a sentence in a supplier questionnaire.

**We nonetheless advise holding all external communications to the B2C standard, for five practical reasons:**

1. **You cannot segment a website.** A public page, careers page, blog or social post is reachable by consumers whatever audience you intended. The distinction protects a tender document, not a homepage.
2. **B2B misleading advertising is separately regulated** under the Misleading and Comparative Advertising Directive (2006/114/EC) and national unfair-competition law, which supply a cause of action to competitors.
3. **Your enterprise customers impose the standard contractually**, because their own reporting depends on the integrity of what you tell them.
4. **Consistency risk.** Where a customer in scope of CSRD relies on your figures, a claim of yours that conflicts with their reported data becomes their problem and then, contractually, yours.
5. **Maintaining two standards fails in practice.** Copy migrates from a deck to a page to an ad. One standard, applied everywhere, is cheaper to operate than a boundary nobody remembers.

**Finally, the exception that defines your strategy.** The framework leaves fully intact claims grounded in **verified actual emission reductions within your own value chain, documented with audit-ready Scope 1, 2 and 3 data**. That is why Rule 3 and section 3.3 are drafted as they are: EU law has closed the route that runs through buying something, and left open the route that runs through measuring, reducing and evidencing. You have the data to take the open route. Most of your competitors do not.

The pressure to approve a marginal claim always arrives with a deadline attached, usually from someone senior, usually for a launch. Nothing in EU law requires you to have a sustainability narrative — it only governs the one you choose to publish. Saying less, more precisely, is always available.
